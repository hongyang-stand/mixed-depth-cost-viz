Mixed depth with one or two layers per cell size is an estimated 16–18% faster per training step than halo

FireNet-2Hz native-resolution stack. Model estimate from 80 production FDS decks and the measured H100 step profile; not yet benchmarked. The convolution schemes are illustrated in the toy figures (halo = C, mixed depth = I).

In mixed depth, big cells hear from the fine region only after it is pooled to their size

A 2 m × 4 m slice across a seam: 1 m cells on the left, 0.25 m cells on the right. Arrows are reads across the seam within a layer. Halo reads both ways at every layer. Mixed depth reads only fine-from-coarse at fine resolution; the two sides first exchange both ways at 1 m, after the fine cells are pooled twice.

Halo: the same at each of its 4 native layers

Mixed depth: one pass up, one pass down

For a waiting 1 m cell to be readable at the 0.25 m level it must already be in feature space, so the shared input encoder runs on every cell first, as a pointwise (1×1) layer that mixes nothing across sizes.

Mixed depth wins by running fewer layers per level, not fewer rows

Native-stack work, in units of "halo's corners × one 32-channel layer", split by cell size. Halo runs 4 layers on every corner. Mixed depth runs each level's layers only on the cells at that level (native plus pooled finer cells), so its rows total 1.14× halo's: it saves time only when each level runs fewer layers. Native 1 m cells are ~42% of corners, so widening the coarse levels is expensive.

Pre-build checks from the same decks

Every FDS mesh is 16³ cells; pooling lines up (cell counts divisible, origins on the level lattice) in decks. Pooled blocks re-tile into full 16³ tiles for most cells, which keeps the halo ring near halo's 0.40 slots per row.

Design and method

  • Shared weights across cell sizes. One set of native conv weights for 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 m, told the cell size by log2(dx) at the input (constant over a level, so a free per-level bias). A pointwise input encoder runs on every cell first, so waiting cells can be read through the halo, and a per-level scale and shift (FiLM) after each LayerNorm. The dense 4 / 16 / 64 m levels keep their own weights.
  • Where the saving comes from. Halo's per-layer gather averages fine corners into coarse ones at every size change ( of its gather nonzeros); mixed depth never needs that. The 4 m pool reads the 2 m level (0.17 of the rows) instead of every corner. The rest is fewer layers per row.
  • Method. Rows, halo slots and halo nonzeros counted from the &MESH lines of 80 production decks. Each category of the measured H100 profile (median validation deck, 2.13 M corners) is scaled by its own count: convolutions by rows × input × output channels, LayerNorm / GELU / elementwise / copy by rows × channels (80% assumed native), cuSPARSE by nonzeros × channels. Wall time outside those categories is held fixed.
  • Not modelled. Per-level kernel launch overhead; the 0.5 m level's small size (0.12 of the rows) may not fill the GPU; memory. The next step is a one-hour H100 benchmark of halo against M3 in bf16.
